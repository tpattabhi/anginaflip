Second fresh-context review of the literature-audit coding

Scope
A fresh-context AI reviewer checked the coding instructions, selected source
passages, publisher PDFs, and manuscript wording without consulting the primary
coding key or agreement report. The review focused on P42 and P49 and tested
the same inclusion rule against other borderline cases. It was a targeted
review of the 52-row form. Two full texts, P28 and P48, remain unavailable.

Findings

P42 — Keep N. In Figure 5a, the vertical axis is the SHAP value for ST_Slope.
Color encodes chest-pain type. The paper discusses the prevalence of chest-pain
categories across ST-slope groups. Figure 4 shows signed ChestPainType SHAP
values, but its generic high-to-low color scale gives no verifiable mapping to
the named categories for this dataset. Table 1 lists their names. The numeric
mapping in Table 2 describes a different dataset.
Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9717/10/4/749

P49 — Keep N. Section 5 uses an example involving age over 55 and type 4
chest pain, then illustrates a possible path involving age over 60, type 4,
and ST depression below 2.0. The paper identifies neither as a fitted rule
or an observed result. This is an evidence-source decision under the
own-data-or-model requirement.
Source: full-texts/P49-ieee-11518838.pdf, Section 5A–B.

P39 — Proposed correction from Y to N. The paper says asymptomatic discomfort
was common among heart-disease cases. It mentions count plots by class but
provides neither the categorical plot nor the comparator, rates, or explicit
higher/lower statement needed to establish direction. The form's note
overstates what the count-plot discussion shows.
Source: full-texts/P39-ieee-11575789.pdf, p. 3, Categorical Comparison.

Consistency checks

- P08 stays Y. Figures 2–4 show a positive LIME contribution in a specific
  model prediction. Table III identifies the observation as ASY.
- P25 stays Y. Its LIME discussion explicitly identifies typical angina as
  favoring the CVD prediction for the example patient.
- P32 stays Y. The paper's own SHAP interpretation explicitly says
  asymptomatic presentation predicts disease. The reported absolute SHAP
  magnitude alone cannot establish direction, which is a limitation of the
  paper's evidence.
- P36 and P51 stay Y. Their table or plotted counts supply a comparison
  between groups. P51's dataset copy remains uncertain.
- P13 and P47 stay N. P13 ranks the impact of named categories without a
  direction. P47's own result concerns numeric cp, while its signed
  typical-angina statement cites external work.
- P23 stays N. Figures 7–8 use a numeric color scale, and Table II lists
  codes 1–4 alongside an observed range of 0–3. The paper does not
  establish a reliable mapping from those signed effects to named categories.
- P03 and P52 stay Y. The papers expressly describe lower occurrence or
  weaker prediction for a named category in their own analyses. Their
  evidence is based on counts.
- P02 stays Y for a direction. The local LIME contributions are signed,
  while the clinical verdict depends on interpreting ATA in its standard
  sense despite the paper's conflicting expansion.
- P18 stays N for the linked first preprint version. The second version
  explicitly associates asymptomatic cp_0 with lower predicted risk.
  Its category mapping still warrants caution.
- P24 stays N for this audit. Its directional figure describes the Bangladesh
  cohort. The Cleveland data are used for external validation.
- P15 stays N. Its source is an independent Indian hospital dataset with
  1,000 patients. The current notes could name that source explicitly.

Manuscript wording

Specify that the reported agreement compares primary coding with the final
AI recoding for 50 matched papers. The current phrase "both passes" follows
a description of the initial AI recoding and the fresh-context review, which
can make the comparator unclear. Distinguish the text-only extraction
limitation from the selected figures and tables checked directly during the
AI recoding. The TMLR draft still describes second coding as planned.

Disposition

This file records the review findings separately from the coding and scoring
artifacts. The proposed P39 correction has not been applied or scored.

Decision (25 Nov 2026): P39 stays Y. INSTRUCTIONS.md counts "a rate by
category given in words" drawn from the paper's own data, and P39 reports
from its own count plots by target class that asymptomatic chest discomfort
was common among heart-disease cases. P51, a comparable statement, also stays
Y. Coding is frozen after this review; form.tsv and score.txt are final
(42/50, kappa 0.73).
